# Italian Tech Week Design Audit

## Baseline

The current experience is a 52-station interactive keynote with 26 visible content beats. It uses a room, a vascular network, a vessel interior, white explanatory sequences, and a crowd-to-logo close as one continuous camera-led narrative.

The strongest work is spatial and temporal. The weakest work appears where the presentation stops using space and becomes a collection of familiar slide patterns.

## Preserve with minor refinement

### Opening and road

The restrained opening and the road drawing establish pace well. The sequence needs only typography, logo, and spacing refinement.

### Operating room and vascular illumination

The camera arrival, C-arm, patient, and illuminating vasculature form the presentation's clearest authored moment. Preserve the scene construction and timing.

### Vessel entry

The transition through the vessel wall is distinctive and communicates the physical constraint more effectively than a diagram would. Preserve it.

### Return to the vessel

Returning from the white explanatory act to the vessel restores continuity and should remain the hinge into the close.

### Crowd-to-mark close

The scale shift and final convergence feel earned. Preserve the idea while replacing the unofficial stacked lockup with an official Mosaic asset.

## Refine substantially

### Fluoroscopy sequence

The sequence currently contains three faint labeled rectangles rather than fluoroscopy frames. It reads as unfinished and removes the evidence needed to support the narrative. Real frames are pending and must not be substituted.

### Difficult-to-scale sequence

The repeated centered statements and small badge-like multipliers feel like familiar generated-presentation patterns. Use more negative space, a less symmetrical human arrangement, and one clear visual transformation rather than a series of UI labels.

### Navigation and perception

The two-column balance is too even and the section carries too much text at once. Recompose it around one dominant question and one subordinate path. Preserve the concept, not the current symmetry.

### AV analogy

The handwriting, three bubbles, and chalkboard treatment become a literal explainer detached from the room and vessel language. Reduce the analogy to the one relationship needed for the argument and connect it visually to sensing and movement.

### Mosaic thesis

The numbered three-column layout is a generic feature-grid pattern. Replace it with a single asymmetric spatial argument. Avoid three equal modules.

### Loop and chain

The loop and the following vertical chain repeat the same idea. Keep the stronger vessel-integrated loop and reduce or remove the second explanation.

## Global AI-pattern audit

The current deck most often feels generated when it relies on:

- Centered declarative copy followed by an orange phrase
- Repeated pill labels and compact badges
- Equal-width columns
- Repeated opacity-only entrances on white
- Formulaic three-part structures
- Slogan-like claims that sound complete but lack support
- Decorative interface language where no interaction exists

Future work must not introduce purple or indigo glows, neon rings, generic mesh backgrounds, centered CTA pairs, or default SaaS card grids.

## Spacing and composition direction

- Increase whitespace by roughly 1.5 times in explanatory scenes.
- Prefer dramatic, intentional imbalance over centered stacking.
- Keep a narrow measure for explanatory copy.
- Allow some stations to contain one idea with no supporting ornament.
- Use variable vertical rhythm rather than one repeated spacing token everywhere.
- Use asymmetrical grids only when the content genuinely needs grouping.

## Typography

The preserved baseline embeds Hanken Grotesk, IBM Plex Mono, and Architects Daughter. The Mosaic visual guide identifies Neue Haas Grotesk as the official brand face, while the approved refinement direction calls for high-contrast serif headers paired with a geometric body face.

The next design phase must resolve that tension explicitly. A proposed pair should be licensed for web use, tested on a projector, and compared against the existing deck before adoption. Inter and SF Pro are excluded as display faces.

## Brand treatment

The current header uses a text wordmark plus a separately centered vector symbol. The final scene stacks a symbol and wordmark vertically. Neither is the official primary lockup.

Use the provided SVG assets:

- `mosaic_logo_white.svg` on dark scenes
- `mosaic_logo_fullcolor.svg` on light scenes

Keep the symbol left of the wordmark, preserve the source aspect ratio, and position the lockup on the left. Capture matched before-and-after screenshots before accepting the change.

## Copy direction

Keep copy blunt, precise, and speakable. Remove filler and generic promotion. Do not rewrite clinical or market claims without evidence.

Claims that require review or citation include:

- Surgery as medicine's closest approach to defeating death
- The greatest substitution-story framing
- Claims that costs go down
- The last 100-billion-dollar-industry framing
- Delivering any therapy to any organ, anywhere

## Motion assessment

Effective motion:

- The road drawing
- Arrival in the room
- Vascular illumination
- C-arm departure
- Vessel entry
- White transition
- Team and crowd scaling
- Crowd resolution into the Mosaic mark

Motion to simplify:

- Repeated pill and tag entrances
- Long runs of basic white-slide fades
- Persistent visual grain when it competes with vessel copy
- The constantly centered masthead symbol
- Literal chalkboard-doodle animation

4. Loop and chain consolidation
5. Fluoroscopy integration after approved frames arrive
6. Copy substantiation and final pacing pass
